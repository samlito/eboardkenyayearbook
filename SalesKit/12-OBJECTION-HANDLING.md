## HOW TO USE THIS GUIDE

Every objection is a buying signal. When a prospect raises an objection, it means they are engaged enough to think critically about the solution. Your role is not to argue — it is to understand the real concern behind the stated objection and address it with empathy, evidence, and a question that moves the conversation forward.

**The LAER Framework:**
- **Listen** — Let the prospect finish. Do not interrupt or become defensive.
- **Acknowledge** — Validate the concern. "That is a very reasonable concern" or "I hear this often."
- **Explore** — Ask a clarifying question to understand the real issue behind the stated objection.
- **Respond** — Address the real concern with specific E-Board capabilities and a follow-up question.

## OBJECTION 1: "WE ALREADY USE EMAIL FOR BOARD PAPERS"

### What the Prospect Really Means
"Our current process works well enough. I do not see the need to change."

They are not saying email is better — they are saying change is risky and disruptive. They may also be unaware of the governance risks in email-based board paper distribution.

### Recommended Response
[READY TO USE]

> "Email is how most institutions start, and it works — up to a point. But let me ask you: when a board paper is revised after you have emailed it to fifteen board members, how do you ensure every member is reading the correct version? And when a regulatory body asks you to demonstrate who accessed a confidential paper, can email provide that audit trail?"

> "E-Board does not replace email — it actually sends email notifications automatically at every workflow stage. What it adds is version control, access tracking, and a single source of truth. Board members still receive an email notification, but instead of opening a 50MB attachment, they click a link to access the latest version in a secure, branded portal."

> "The institutions that benefit most from E-Board are the ones that have outgrown email — where the volume, the sensitivity, or the regulatory requirements have made email insufficient."

### Follow-Up Question
> "When was the last time a board member referenced the wrong version of a paper during a meeting? How was that resolved?"

## OBJECTION 2: "WE USE WHATSAPP FOR BOARD COMMUNICATION"

### What the Prospect Really Means
"WhatsApp is convenient and board members are already on it. Anything else adds friction."

This often signals that the institution has prioritised convenience over governance. The prospect may not have considered the compliance and security implications.

### Recommended Response
[READY TO USE]

> "WhatsApp is excellent for quick, informal communication — and I understand the appeal. Board members are already on it, there is no training needed, and it is instant. But there are three governance concerns worth considering."

> "First, WhatsApp conversations are stored on personal devices. When a board member's phone is lost, stolen, or accessed by a family member, your board's confidential deliberations are exposed. Second, WhatsApp has no access control — once a document is shared in a group, any member can forward it to anyone. Third, there is no audit trail that meets governance standards. If an oversight body asks you to demonstrate how a decision was communicated and who received it, WhatsApp chat logs are not a defensible record."

> "E-Board provides the same convenience — board members receive notifications on their devices and can access papers from anywhere — but within a governance framework that protects the institution."

### Follow-Up Question
> "If a sensitive board matter — say, a personnel decision or a legal issue — were to be leaked from a WhatsApp group, how would your institution trace the source or demonstrate that appropriate controls were in place?"

## OBJECTION 25: "WE ARE CONCERNED ABOUT DATA PRIVACY AND GDPR / DATA PROTECTION"

### What the Prospect Really Means
"We handle personal data of board members and sensitive governance information. We need to comply with data protection regulations."

### Recommended Response
[READY TO USE]

> "Data privacy is a critical concern, and Kenya's Data Protection Act of 2019 applies to the processing of personal data by institutions like yours. E-Board is designed with data protection principles in mind."

> "The platform processes board member personal data — names, contacts, qualifications, appointment details — solely for the purpose of governance administration. Access to this data is controlled through role-based permissions, and the comprehensive audit trail documents exactly who accessed what data and when."

> "Board member profiles, documents, and communications are accessible only to authorised users based on their roles and permissions. The three-level document visibility system — all members, committee-only, or specific individuals — provides additional data access control."

> "Account status management ensures that when a board member's term ends, their access is deactivated. OTP-based authentication protects against unauthorised account access."

[REQUIRES LOSVINCA INPUT — Confirm data protection compliance documentation: privacy policy, data processing agreements, data retention policies, data subject access request procedures, and data centre location relative to Kenya's Data Protection Act requirements]

### Follow-Up Question
> "Does your institution have a Data Protection Officer? It would be valuable to involve them in our discussion so we can address any specific data protection requirements they may have."

## OBJECTION 26: "THE PRICE IS TOO HIGH"

### What the Prospect Really Means
Either (a) genuine budget constraints, (b) they do not yet see sufficient value, or (c) they are negotiating.

### Recommended Response
[READY TO USE]

> "I want to make sure we are comparing like with like. Let me break down what E-Board replaces:"

> "**Printing and binding:** If your institution prints board packs for each meeting — say, twelve meetings per year, fifteen board members, two hundred pages per pack — that is thirty-six thousand pages of printing per year, plus binding, plus supplementary papers when revisions happen."

> "**Courier and distribution:** Physical delivery of board packs to board members' offices or residences."

> "**Staff time:** The hours your Corporation Secretary's team spends on formatting minutes, tracking paper versions, compiling audit records, and following up on resolutions."

> "**Risk mitigation:** The cost of a governance failure — an audit finding, a legal challenge to a decision, a confidential document leaked — can be orders of magnitude higher than any software investment."

> "When you total these costs, E-Board typically pays for itself within the first year — often within the first few months."

> "That said, if the annual commitment is a concern, our pilot programme lets you start with a shorter engagement to prove the value before committing to a full subscription."

[REQUIRES LOSVINCA INPUT — Specific pricing tiers, ROI calculator, and approved discount structures]

### Follow-Up Question
> "Would it be helpful if we worked through a specific cost comparison using your institution's actual numbers — meeting frequency, board pack sizes, current printing and distribution costs?"
