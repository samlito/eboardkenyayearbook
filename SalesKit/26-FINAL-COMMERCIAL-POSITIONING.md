## 1. LOSVINCA'S RECOMMENDED MARKET POSITIONING

### Position: Africa's Specialist Board Governance Technology Company

Losvinca should position itself not as a general software company, but as a specialist firm focused exclusively on board governance technology for institutions with formal governance structures.

**Why this positioning works:**

- **Specialist credibility:** Institutions trust domain experts over generalists. A company dedicated to board governance understands the compliance requirements, procedural complexities, and stakeholder dynamics that generic software firms do not.

- **Defensible niche:** The intersection of board governance and technology in Africa is underserved. By claiming this niche early, Losvinca builds brand association with the category before larger players enter.

- **Premium pricing justification:** Specialist solutions command higher prices than generic tools repurposed for governance. Organisations expect to pay more for purpose-built software that understands their regulatory environment.

- **Referral network effect:** Corporation Secretaries, governance professionals, and board members operate within tight professional networks (ICPSK, IoD-K, professional associations). A strong specialist reputation in this community creates a self-reinforcing referral loop.

**Positioning statement for internal use:**

> Losvinca is Africa's specialist board governance technology company. We build purpose-built digital solutions that help institutions run compliant, efficient, and transparent board operations — from the first notice to the final resolution.

## 8. RECOMMENDED SALES STRATEGY

### Strategy: Pilot-Led Institutional Sales

Losvinca should adopt a **pilot-led sales strategy** as its primary go-to-market approach. This means every sales engagement is oriented toward getting E-Board into the organisation's hands through a structured pilot, rather than selling on presentations and promises alone.

**Strategy components:**

### 8.1 Lead with Relationships, Not Technology

In the Kenyan institutional market, purchasing decisions are relationship-driven. Cold technology pitches rarely work. Every engagement should begin with a genuine conversation about the prospect's governance challenges, not a product demonstration.

**Tactical approach:**
- Target Corporation Secretaries as primary entry points (they own the governance problem)
- Build relationships at ICPSK events, IoD-K forums, and governance conferences
- Offer value before asking for anything: share governance insights, Mwongozo updates, best practices
- Position Losvinca as a governance partner, not a software vendor

### 8.2 Demonstrate, Don't Describe

Institutional buyers need to see and touch the solution. Abstract feature lists do not close deals. Every qualified prospect should experience a tailored demo within 2 weeks of first meaningful contact.

**Tactical approach:**
- Customise every demo with the prospect's logo, colours, and board structure
- Walk through a complete meeting lifecycle relevant to their context
- Show, do not tell: demonstrate audit trail, digital signatures, conflict of interest workflow
- Always end a demo with an invitation to pilot

### 8.3 Convert Through Pilots

The pilot is the single most important conversion tool. Once a Corporation Secretary runs a real board meeting on E-Board, the switching cost of going back to manual processes becomes apparent.

**Tactical approach:**
- Offer 30-60 day no-cost pilots for qualified prospects
- Define pilot scope clearly: 1-2 committees, 2-3 meetings
- Provide hands-on support during pilot (do not leave them alone with the system)
- Ensure at least one complete meeting cycle is completed
- Document pilot results and present to decision-makers

### 8.4 Build the Reference Network

In institutional markets, references close deals. Every satisfied customer should be converted into a reference and, ideally, an advocate.

**Tactical approach:**
- Ask for testimonials and references at every successful touchpoint
- Develop case studies with customer permission
- Facilitate peer-to-peer conversations (existing customer to prospect)
- Invite customers to speak at webinars and events

### 8.5 Align with Procurement Realities

Government procurement is structured, cyclical, and slow. Fighting the system is futile; working within it is essential.

**Tactical approach:**
- Understand each prospect's procurement method (direct, RFQ, tender)
- Time proposals to budget cycles (preparation begins October-January for July-June FY)
- Maintain all required statutory documents current
- Build relationships with procurement officers, not just end users
- Be patient — government deals take 4-12 months but are sticky once landed

## 9. RECOMMENDED FIRST 3 CUSTOMER SEGMENTS TO TARGET

### Segment 1: State Corporations and Parastatals (Priority: Highest)

**Why target first:**
- Mandated to comply with Mwongozo Code of Governance — E-Board directly addresses this compliance requirement
- Have formal board structures with Corporation Secretaries, making them ideal users
- Operate on the July-June financial year that E-Board is built around
- Face regular governance audits by the Auditor General — audit trail is a critical need
- Budget for ICT solutions exists in most state corporations
- Approximately 300+ state corporations and state agencies in Kenya

**Target profile:**
- State corporations with boards of 5-15 members
- Have a designated Corporation Secretary
- Currently using manual or semi-manual governance processes
- Have experienced governance audit findings or compliance pressure

**Entry point:** Corporation Secretary, with engagement of CEO and ICT Head

**Initial target list:** 15-20 state corporations across key sectors (health, water, energy, agriculture, education, transport)

---

### Segment 2: Independent Regulatory Bodies and Commissions (Priority: High)

**Why target second:**
- Similar compliance requirements to state corporations
- Generally have well-structured boards with clear governance mandates
- Tend to be early technology adopters (smaller, more agile than large ministries)
- Strong reputational motivation for governance excellence (regulators must lead by example)
- Concentrated in Nairobi, reducing logistics costs
- Approximately 50-70 regulatory bodies and constitutional commissions in Kenya

**Target profile:**
- Regulatory authorities (CMA, CBK, ERC, EPRA, CAK, etc.)
- Constitutional commissions (EACC, SRC, NLC, etc.)
- Agencies with multiple committees requiring governance support

**Entry point:** Corporation Secretary or CEO

**Initial target list:** 10-15 regulatory bodies and commissions

---

### Segment 3: Large NGOs and International Organisations (Priority: High)

**Why target alongside government:**
- Faster procurement cycles than government (30-90 days vs. 90-240 days)
- Provides early revenue while government pipeline matures
- Strong governance culture driven by donor accountability requirements
- International organisations often have bigger budgets and willingness to pay for governance tools
- Board governance is a condition of donor funding for many NGOs
- Hundreds of large NGOs and international organisations operating in Kenya

**Target profile:**
- International NGOs with Kenya offices and local boards or advisory committees
- Large national NGOs with formal governance structures
- UN agencies and multilateral organisations with governance committees
- Membership-based professional bodies with elected boards

**Entry point:** CEO, Country Director, or Head of Operations

**Initial target list:** 15-20 large NGOs and international organisations
