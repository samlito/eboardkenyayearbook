## SLIDE 2: THE GOVERNANCE CHALLENGE

**Title:** Board Governance Demands Are Increasing. The Tools Have Not Kept Up.

**Body Content:**

Governance expectations for public institutions are rising:

- **Regulatory scrutiny** — Oversight bodies require documented evidence of governance processes, decision-making trails, and compliance with established standards
- **Board complexity** — Multiple committees, increasing volumes of board papers, and growing regulatory requirements
- **Accountability demands** — Stakeholders expect board decisions to be tracked from resolution to implementation
- **Audit readiness** — Internal and external auditors require retrievable, verifiable governance records

Yet most institutions still manage these demands using:

- Microsoft Word and email for board papers
- Printed packs delivered by courier
- Paper sign-in sheets and declaration forms
- Spreadsheets for resolution tracking
- Physical filing cabinets for governance records

**The gap between governance expectations and governance tools is where risk lives.**

**Speaker Notes:**
Let me start with the context. Governance requirements for public institutions — state corporations, government agencies, regulatory bodies — are more demanding than ever. Oversight bodies want documented evidence. Auditors want retrievable records. Stakeholders want to see that board decisions actually translate into action. These are reasonable expectations. The problem is that the tools most institutions use to manage governance have not changed in decades. Board papers are still prepared in Word, emailed around for review with no version control, printed in multiple copies, bound, and physically delivered. Attendance is captured on a sign-in sheet. Conflict of interest is a paper form — if it is captured at all. Resolutions are written in the minutes and then... often, nobody tracks whether they were actually implemented. This gap between what governance demands and what your tools can deliver is where institutional risk accumulates.

## SLIDE 3: HOW ORGANISATIONS MANAGE BOARDS TODAY

**Title:** The Manual Board Meeting Lifecycle

**Body Content — show as a process flow with pain points:**

| Stage | Current Practice | Pain Point |
|---|---|---|
| **Planning** | Dates agreed via email and phone | No central almanac; scheduling conflicts |
| **Paper Preparation** | Drafted in Word, emailed to reviewers | Multiple versions; no audit trail |
| **Paper Review** | Email chains, verbal feedback | No structured approval workflow |
| **Notice** | Email to board members | No receipt confirmation; notice compliance uncertain |
| **Distribution** | Printed, bound, couriered | High cost; delays; last-minute changes impossible |
| **Meeting Day** | Paper sign-in, paper COI forms | Incomplete records; no verification |
| **Minutes** | Typed from handwritten notes days later | Delayed; accuracy concerns |
| **Resolutions** | Listed in minutes, tracked in spreadsheet | Low follow-through; no accountability |
| **Document Storage** | Filing cabinets, shared drives, email | Difficult retrieval; risk of loss |

**Speaker Notes:**
Let me make this concrete. This is what we see when we sit down with Corporation Secretaries and governance teams across institutions. Meeting dates are set informally. Board papers go through an unstructured review process over email — and if you have ever tried to figure out which version of a board paper is the final version, you understand the problem. Notices go out by email with no way to confirm they were received within the required timeframe. Papers are printed — sometimes hundreds of pages per member — bound, and physically delivered. On meeting day, attendance is a sign-in sheet, and conflict of interest is a paper form that may or may not be completed by every member. Minutes are typed up days or weeks later from handwritten notes. And resolutions — the actual decisions of the board — are captured in the minutes and then tracked, if they are tracked at all, in a spreadsheet that one person maintains. This is not a criticism of the people doing this work. They are working hard with inadequate tools.

## SLIDE 6: HOW E-BOARD WORKS — THE 10-STAGE WORKFLOW

**Title:** The Complete Board Meeting Lifecycle in One Platform

**Body Content — visual workflow diagram:**

```
1. MEETING INITIATION
   ↓
2. INTERNAL NOTICE
   ↓
3. CEO APPROVAL
   ↓
4. OFFICER ASSIGNMENTS
   ↓
5. PAPER PREPARATION
   ↓
6. DIRECTOR REVIEW
   ↓
7. CEO REVIEW
   ↓
8. AGENDA BUILDING
   ↓
9. LIVE MEETING
   (Attendance + Conflict of Interest with OTP + Digital Signature)
   ↓
10. MINUTES
    ↓
    RESOLUTIONS → Assignment → Tracking → Completion
```

**At every stage:**
- Automated email notifications (33+ templates)
- In-app notifications with read/unread tracking
- Complete audit trail with before/after values
- Role-based visibility and permissions

**Speaker Notes:**
Here is the complete workflow. Every board meeting in E-Board moves through 10 defined stages. It starts with meeting initiation — creating the meeting record and linking it to your approved almanac. Internal notices go to the officers responsible for preparing materials. The CEO approves the meeting. Officers are assigned their responsibilities. Board papers are prepared, submitted for director review, then advanced to CEO review. The agenda is built from the approved papers. On meeting day, the platform manages the live meeting — digital attendance, conflict of interest declarations verified by OTP and captured with digital signatures. Minutes are captured during the meeting. And after the meeting, resolutions are created, assigned to responsible parties with due dates, and tracked through to completion. At every stage, the platform sends automated notifications — there are over 33 email templates built in — and every action is recorded in the audit trail. Nothing falls through the cracks because the workflow does not allow it.
