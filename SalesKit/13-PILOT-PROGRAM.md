## SECTION A: PILOT PROGRAMME DESIGN

### Programme Rationale

Government institutions, state corporations, and NGOs operate within formal procurement frameworks that require evidence-based decision-making. A pilot programme serves three purposes:

1. **Proof of Value** — The institution experiences E-Board in a real governance scenario before committing to a full subscription.
2. **Internal Consensus** — The pilot generates tangible evidence (a completed digital meeting cycle) that stakeholders can evaluate.
3. **Budget Justification** — Pilot results provide the data needed to justify a full budget allocation, particularly when the pilot occurs in one financial year and the full subscription is budgeted for the next.

### Programme Parameters

| Parameter | Specification |
|-----------|--------------|
| **Duration** | 30 calendar days |
| **Users** | Up to 25 users (sufficient for a full board plus administrative staff) |
| **Meetings** | At least 1 complete meeting cycle (recommended: 1 board meeting or 1 committee meeting) |
| **Modules Included** | All E-Board modules — full platform access, no feature restrictions |
| **Branding** | Full white-label configuration with institution's identity |
| **Support** | Dedicated implementation support throughout the pilot |
| **Data** | All data created during the pilot is retained if the institution converts to a full subscription |

### Training Approach

**Principle:** Train users on the specific tasks they perform in their role. Do not train board members on administrative functions, and do not train officers on CEO-level approvals.

| Role | Training Focus | Duration | Format |
|------|---------------|----------|--------|
| System Administrator | Full platform configuration, user management, permissions, email setup, audit trail | 2 hours | On-site or remote |
| Corporation Secretary | Almanac, meeting initiation, notices, agenda building, minutes capture, resolutions, conflict register, PDF exports | 3 hours | On-site or remote |
| Officers | Board paper preparation, annexure attachment, status tracking, notifications | 2 hours | On-site or remote |
| Directors/Division Heads | Paper review, approval workflow, notifications | 1.5 hours | On-site or remote |
| CEO | Paper review with sticky notes, notice approval, almanac approval, dashboard, resolution overview | 1 hour | On-site (recommended) |
| Board Members | Board portal navigation, paper review, sticky notes, conflict declaration, profile management | 45 minutes | On-site or remote (group session) |

### Conversion to Annual Subscription

**Pilot-to-Subscription Pathway:**

1. **Day 29 — Evaluation Meeting:** Losvinca and the institution review the pilot results against success criteria. Feedback is collected from all user groups.

2. **Day 30 — Conversion Proposal:** Losvinca presents the annual subscription proposal, including:
   - Summary of pilot achievements
   - User feedback highlights
   - Cost-benefit analysis based on actual pilot experience
   - Annual subscription terms and pricing
   - Implementation plan for full rollout (if the pilot covered only one committee)

3. **Post-Pilot Data Continuity:** All data created during the pilot — board member profiles, meeting records, papers, minutes, resolutions, documents, and audit trails — is retained and carries forward into the full subscription. No data is lost, and no re-entry is required.

4. **Procurement Support:** Losvinca provides all documentation required for the institution's formal procurement process, including technical specifications, compliance statements, and pricing in the required format.

## SECTION C: PILOT PROGRAMME SALES GUIDANCE

### When to Propose the Pilot

The pilot programme is your most powerful sales tool in the following situations:

1. **Budget objection** — "We do not have the budget" becomes "Start with a pilot this financial year, use the results to justify a full budget allocation for next year."

2. **Adoption concern** — "Board members will not use it" becomes "Let us prove it with one meeting. If board members do not engage, you have invested very little."

3. **Previous bad experience** — "We tried something before and it failed" becomes "The pilot is exactly thirty days. If E-Board does not deliver, you walk away with no obligation."

4. **Need for internal consensus** — "I need management approval" becomes "The pilot gives you tangible evidence — a completed digital meeting — that you can present to management."

5. **Competitive evaluation** — "We are looking at other solutions" becomes "Run our pilot in parallel with your evaluation. Let the results speak for themselves."

6. **Timing objection** — "The timing is not right" becomes "A pilot requires minimal commitment and can align with your next scheduled committee meeting."

### How to Present the Pilot

> "Rather than asking you to make a decision based on a demonstration, let me suggest something more practical. Our thirty-day pilot programme gives your institution full access to E-Board — every feature, no restrictions — to conduct a real board or committee meeting digitally. You experience the entire workflow: from meeting initiation and board paper preparation, through the review process and notice distribution, to the meeting itself with digital attendance and conflict declarations, and finally minutes capture and resolution tracking."

> "At the end of thirty days, you will have concrete evidence — not a vendor's promises, but your own experience — of what digital board governance looks like for your institution. If it works, we transition seamlessly to an annual subscription with all your data intact. If it does not meet your expectations, you walk away with no obligation."

> "Would it make sense to align the pilot with your next scheduled committee meeting?"

### Pilot Qualification Questions

Before proposing a pilot, ensure the institution can deliver on its side:

1. **"Do you have a board or committee meeting scheduled within the next six weeks?"** — The pilot needs a real meeting to demonstrate value.

2. **"Can you identify a Corporation Secretary or governance officer who will champion this internally?"** — Without an internal champion, the pilot will not receive the attention it needs.

3. **"Are your board members willing to participate in a forty-five-minute orientation session?"** — Board member participation is essential.

4. **"Can your institution provide branding assets — logo, letterhead, CEO signature — within the first week?"** — Configuration cannot begin without these.

5. **"Is there a decision-maker who will evaluate the pilot results and can authorise conversion to a full subscription?"** — Ensure the pilot leads to a decision, not indefinite evaluation.

### Common Pilot Risks and Mitigation

| Risk | Mitigation |
|------|-----------|
| No meeting falls within the 30-day window | Align pilot start date with the institution's meeting calendar. If necessary, use a committee meeting rather than a full board meeting. |
| Board members do not attend orientation | Schedule the orientation at a time convenient for board members. Offer remote sessions. Provide written quick-reference guides as a fallback. |
| Corporation Secretary is too busy to manage the pilot | Assign a dedicated support staff member as the day-to-day pilot coordinator. Losvinca provides additional hands-on support. |
| CEO does not engage with approval workflows | Schedule the CEO training session first, before other users. Keep the CEO session to one hour maximum. Emphasise the dashboard and sticky notes — features that deliver personal value. |
| Institution requests pilot extension | Offer a one-time extension of up to 14 days if the meeting was delayed. Use the extension to strengthen the conversion conversation. |
| Pilot succeeds but procurement delays conversion | Maintain the pilot instance in a read-only state while procurement proceeds. Provide all procurement documentation proactively. |

## SECTION E: FEATURES NOT INCLUDED IN THE PILOT

The following capabilities are **NOT CURRENTLY AVAILABLE** in E-Board and should not be promised or implied during the pilot programme. These are documented as potential future enhancements:

- SMS notifications to board members
- Video conferencing integration for remote meetings
- Board performance evaluations
- Electronic voting during meetings
- External calendar synchronisation (Google Calendar, Outlook)
- Native mobile application (iOS or Android)
- Advanced analytics or business intelligence dashboards
- REST API integrations with third-party systems (ERP, HRIS, etc.)
- User-manageable document templates
- Dedicated action points module (separate from resolutions)

If a prospect asks about any of these during the pilot, the recommended response is:

> "That capability is not currently available in E-Board, but it is something we are aware of as a potential future enhancement. Let me note your interest, as it helps us prioritise our product development roadmap. In the meantime, let me show you how E-Board handles [related available capability] today."
